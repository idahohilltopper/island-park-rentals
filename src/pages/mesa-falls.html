Mesa Falls, Idaho: Upper and Lower Falls visitor guide

Short answer: Mesa Falls are two waterfalls on the Henrys Fork of the Snake River, east of US 20 and north of Ashton, Idaho. Upper Mesa Falls drops about 114 feet and Lower Mesa Falls about 65 to 70 feet. Both sit along the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway (Idaho Highway 47), a drive of roughly 29 to 30 miles that makes an easy day trip from Island Park.

Construction update (reported through September 2026): the Forest Service closed the Upper Mesa Falls recreation area on August 10, 2026 for road, trail and boardwalk work. News reports say the closure was expected to last through the winter, and that the goal is to reopen the facilities in spring. A September 17 report said Lower Mesa Falls was also closed during construction. We do not have a reopening date. Please check with the Caribou-Targhee National Forest before you drive out, and do not enter closed areas. Sources: East Idaho News, Aug. 5 and East Idaho News, Sept. 17.

The two falls

The byway starts near Ashton, which has our full area guide.

  • Upper Mesa Falls: about 114 feet tall, with a straight-crested drop. It is roughly 16 miles from Ashton.
  • Lower Mesa Falls: about 65 to 70 feet, with a horseshoe-shaped crest.

Mesa Falls Scenic Byway

The byway follows Idaho Highway 47 through the Caribou-Targhee National Forest. Published figures put it at about 28.7 to 30 miles. Visit Idaho describes the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway as a road trip that pairs well with a Yellowstone visit.

Winter access

Winter turns Mesa Falls into an adventure. According to Idaho sources, the falls are reached by snowmobile or cross-country skis from Bear Gulch on Scenic Highway 47, a trip of roughly 5 miles. Check current conditions before you set out. See winter in Island Park.

Before you go

  • Check current status. Fees, visitor-center hours and closures for maintenance have changed in the past, and sources disagree on the details. Use the Idaho Parks and Recreation Mesa Falls page and the Caribou-Targhee National Forest.
  • Viewing decks can get crowded at busy times, according to visitor guides, so go early.
  • Pair it with other nearby stops: things to do in Island Park, including Big Springs, Harriman State Park and Sawtell Peak.
