South Fork of the Snake River fishing: a visitor's guide

Short answer: The South Fork of the Snake River is a tailwater below Palisades Dam in eastern Idaho, described by Recreation.gov as the largest native cutthroat fishery outside Yellowstone. It is a different river from the Henrys Fork that runs through Island Park, and the two meet near Menan. It makes a great addition to a fishing trip based in Island Park.

The river

The South Fork starts below Palisades Dam near Swan Valley and runs northwest to Menan, where it joins the Henrys Fork. The Bureau of Land Management cites roughly 64 to 66 miles of river, and Idaho Highway 26 follows it. A Visit Idaho article divides the Idaho stretch into the Upper river (the dam to the Swan Valley Bridge), the Canyon and the Lower river. Common access points include Conant, Heise, Byington, Lorenzo and Menan.

The fishing

  • Native Yellowstone cutthroat are the star. Idaho Fish and Game's listing bans harvest of cutthroat trout on the South Fork.
  • Brown and rainbow trout are also in the river.
  • Dry flies. Orvis describes the river as a tailwater with strong dry-fly fishing, with a prime season from mid-May to early November. Fishing is possible year-round.

Rules, boating and access

  • An Idaho fishing license is required. Check the current rules on the South Fork page of the Idaho Fishing Planner.
  • The Bureau of Land Management publishes boating regulations, access sites and maps for the South Fork.
  • Idaho Fish and Game's listing for the river gives a trout limit and the cutthroat harvest ban. Because rules change, verify before you go.
