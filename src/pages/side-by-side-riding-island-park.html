Side-by-side and ATV riding near Island Park

Short answer: Island Park is good side-by-side country, with national forest roads and trails and the St. Anthony Sand Dunes a day trip away. Before you ride, learn three things: Idaho's registration and gear rules, which Forest Service routes allow your vehicle's width, and that the Forest Service's Motor Vehicle Use Map is the legal guide. Our cabin has parking for small to medium trailers.

Idaho rules to know

  • Registration. Idaho Parks and Recreation says nonresident OHV users need an Idaho OHV registration sticker ($12, valid January 1 through December 31), as of January 1, 2020. Other guides say a valid home-state OHV registration may also be accepted, so confirm with the state.
  • County roads. The state says nonresidents who want to ride on county roads need an Idaho restricted vehicle plate with a valid OHV sticker.
  • Gear and equipment. Idaho's OHV booklet lists a DOT-approved helmet for riders under 18, liability insurance, a valid driver's license (with an exception for riders under 16 supervised by a licensed adult), and a muffler and Forest Service approved spark arrestor. Read the current booklet, since rules and fees change.

Riding the national forest

  • Stay on designated routes. The Caribou-Targhee National Forest says its Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM) is a legal document showing which roads and trails are open to public motor vehicle use. If a route does not appear on the map, it is not open.
  • Width classes. The forest says some trails are for 50-inch-wide ATVs and UTVs, some are motorcycle only, and wider roads are for full-size vehicles. Measure your side-by-side before you pick a route.
  • Get the map. The MVUM is free at ranger offices or online, and the interactive version works with the Avenza PDF app.
  • Local maps. The Island Park Chamber lists a regional OHV map. It is a third-party map, so use the Forest Service's MVUM as the legal reference.

The St. Anthony Sand Dunes

For a different kind of day, the BLM-managed St. Anthony Sand Dunes are 10,600 acres of white sand with dunes up to 400 feet. A day-use fee applies, and large parts close in winter. See the guide for fees, camping and closure dates.

Tours and rentals

Local outfitters offer rentals and guided tours in the region, and Yellowstone Teton Territory lists several. The host of our Whitebark Cabin also offers ATV and UTV tours, so ask on the Airbnb listing for current availability.

Ride smart

  • Wear a helmet and eye protection, and ride with a group in remote country.
  • Carry water, a map, a first-aid kit and extra layers. Weather changes quickly at elevation.
  • Respect closures and wildlife, and pack out what you bring in.
  • Check fire restrictions and road conditions before you go. See our conditions page.
