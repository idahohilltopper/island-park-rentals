Buffalo River and Warm River fishing near Island Park

Short answer: The Buffalo River and Warm River are two spring-fed tributaries of the Henrys Fork that offer a quieter alternative to the main river. Warm River is known for fishing well when other waters run high or off-color, and the Buffalo River has a large national forest campground along its banks. You need an Idaho fishing license for both.

Warm River

Idaho Fish and Game describes Warm River as a short, spring-fed tributary of the Henrys Fork that fishes well when other waters are off-color, during runoff and in winter. Recommended species include brown trout, brook trout and mountain whitefish, and catchable rainbow trout have been stocked near the Warm River Campground since the 1960s. See the Warm River page on the Fishing Planner.

  • Where: it joins the Henrys Fork where the Mesa Falls road crosses the river, near the start of the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway.
  • Camping: the Caribou-Targhee National Forest's Warm River Campground sits right on the river and is known for river tubing. Access is from Ashton via Highway 47 and Fish Creek Road.
  • Warm River Cabin: a former hatchery manager's house listed on Recreation.gov, about nine miles northeast of Ashton according to the Forest Service.

Buffalo River

Idaho Fish and Game lists the Buffalo River as a tributary of the Henrys Fork, about 8.7 miles long in Fremont County. Fishbrain lists brook, rainbow and cutthroat trout as the most popular species, and Idaho Fish and Game stocks catchable triploid rainbow trout. See the Buffalo River page on the Fishing Planner. Buffalo Campground sits alongside the river, which is fed by a large spring a few miles upstream, and is close to Henrys Lake State Park and Harriman State Park. See camping in Island Park.

Rules and access

  • Idaho requires a fishing license for anyone 14 or older.
  • Check each water's Special Rules in the current Idaho fishing rules booklet.
  • The Henrys Fork Foundation's FAQ on river access and regulations is a helpful read.
