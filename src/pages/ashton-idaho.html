Ashton, Idaho: gateway to Mesa Falls and Warm River

Short answer: Ashton is a small city in Fremont County, Idaho, with 949 residents at the 2020 census, at about 5,259 feet. It is known for seed potatoes and sits at the junction of two scenic byways. For visitors it is the starting point for Mesa Falls, Warm River and the Ashton-Tetonia Trail, and it is close to Yellowstone and Grand Teton.

Why stop in Ashton

  • Mesa Falls Scenic Byway. The Ashton Chamber says the byway is 28.7 miles and to allow about an hour. Idaho 47 runs northeast from Ashton about 12.4 miles to the old Bear Gulch Ski Area site, then northwest on Forest Service Route 294 to US 20 at the northern end. Upper Mesa Falls is 114 feet tall and Lower Mesa Falls 65 feet. See our Mesa Falls guide. Upper Mesa Falls was reported closed for construction from August 2026, so check the Forest Service before you go.
  • Warm River. About 9 miles from Ashton on Highway 47. See our Warm River guide for the campground, tubing and fishing.
  • Ashton-Tetonia Trail. Visit Idaho describes an almost 30-mile trail that follows the abandoned Union Pacific rail line, packed gravel and dirt, best with tires at least 32 mm wide. It has very limited amenities and is used in winter for Nordic skiing, snowshoeing and snowmobiling.
  • Trout rivers. Wikipedia says four trout rivers are within 10 miles: the Henrys Fork, Fall River, Teton River and Warm River. See our fly fishing guides.
  • Cave Falls. Wikipedia describes Cave Falls on Fall River, about 20 feet tall and 200 feet wide, as the starting point for hikers in the Bechler region of Yellowstone. Check the Forest Service and National Park Service for road access.

Parks nearby

Wikipedia says Ashton is 18 miles from the southwest corner of Yellowstone National Park and 24 miles from Grand Teton National Park. The Ashton Chamber says Ashton is at the junction of the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway and the Teton Scenic Byway. For Yellowstone planning, see our Yellowstone itinerary and Grand Teton and Yellowstone plan.

Events

The Ashton Chamber lists the American Dog Derby, a Mountain Man Rendezvous, a Fourth of July celebration, the Mesa Falls Marathon and Trunk or Treat. The Dog Derby has a long history: Wikipedia says the first race, on March 4, 1917, was a 55-mile run from West Yellowstone to Ashton. We could not find dates, so check the Ashton Chamber of Commerce.

A little history

Wikipedia says Ashton was founded in 1906, the same year the first scheduled train service arrived, and was named for William Ashton, the chief engineer of the Oregon Short Line railroad. Teton Valley branch rail service followed in 1912, and the abandoned railroad grade is now the Ashton-Tetonia Trail. Wikipedia's history section has no citations, so treat the details as a summary.
