Big Springs, Island Park: visitor guide

Short answer: Big Springs is a crystal-clear spring in the Caribou-Targhee National Forest and one of the main sources of the Henrys Fork of the Snake River. It sits about 4.5 miles east of Mack's Inn on US 20, an easy half-day stop from Island Park.

What it is

  • A first-magnitude spring, producing over 120 million gallons of water a day.
  • A primary source of the Henrys Fork (the other major source is the Henrys Lake outlet).
  • A National Natural Landmark, designated in 1980.
  • Known for large rainbow trout that gather near the bridge. Visitors can watch them from above.

How to get there

Recreation.gov gives these directions: from West Yellowstone, Montana, travel 22 miles south on US 20 to Mack's Inn, then turn east on Big Springs Loop Road and drive 4.5 miles. Coming from the south, watch for the Mack's Inn turn on US 20.

What to do

  • Walk the spring trail. A roughly half-mile accessible trail is reported for wildlife viewing, with osprey, bald eagles, waterfowl, moose and deer seen in the area.
  • Visit Johnny Sack Cabin. A log cabin built by Johnny Sack in 1929 stands beside the spring.
  • Float the Water Trail. The Big Springs National Recreation Water Trail runs about 4.5 miles to Mack's Inn, described as a lazy three-hour float. Mack's Inn lists boat rentals and shuttle service.
  • Picnic. The Big Springs Group Area has picnic tables, accessible vault toilets and drinking water.
