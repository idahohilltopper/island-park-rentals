Harriman State Park: visitor guide

Short answer: Harriman State Park is a former ranch and private retreat turned wildlife refuge, with eight miles of the Henrys Fork, about 22 miles of trails and a quiet winter ski season. It sits 28 miles south of West Yellowstone and is one of the best half-day stops near Island Park.

The Railroad Ranch story

From 1902 to 1977 the land was owned by Union Pacific Railroad investors and used as a cattle ranch and private retreat by the Harriman and Guggenheim families. On April 1, 1977, Railroad Ranch became Harriman State Park of Idaho, and it opened to the public in 1982. The historic ranch buildings are still part of the visit.

What to do

  • Wildlife watching. Idaho Parks and Recreation says moose, elk and trumpeter swans are a common sight.
  • Hike, bike or ride. The park lists 22 miles of hiking, mountain biking and horseback trails.
  • Fly fish "the Ranch." About eight miles of the Henrys Fork run through the park, known to anglers worldwide. See our Henrys Fork guide.
  • Ski in winter. Over 33 miles of groomed Nordic trails, plus snowshoeing and fat biking.
  • Stay in a yurt. Yurts are listed for rent in fall and winter. Reserve through Idaho Parks and Recreation.
