Hunting near Island Park, Idaho

Island Park sits in Idaho Fish and Game's Island Park Elk Zone, and much of the surrounding land is national forest. Seasons, tags and quotas change every year, so use the agency's current regulations and hunt planner, not this page, to plan a hunt. Grizzly bears live in the area. Our cabin sleeps 10 and has trailer parking, which suits a hunting camp.

What to check first

  • Idaho Fish and Game. Its hunt planner lists hunts by unit. The agency says the Island Park Elk Zone covers Units 60, 60A, 61, 62 and 62A.
  • Licenses and tags. The agency says a valid hunting license is required for all hunting and trapping, and big game needs the right tag. Nonresident tags have quotas, and the agency lists a nonresident elk tag quota for the Island Park Zone, so check what is available before you travel.
  • Seasons. Dates differ by species, weapon and unit and change every year. Read the current regulations.
  • Hunter education. Check Idaho Fish and Game for current hunter education requirements for you and your group.

Land and access

  • The Forest Service manages most of the land. The agency says it manages about 71 percent of Unit 61, with private land about 17 percent. Confirm ownership and access before you hunt.
  • Check the Caribou-Targhee National Forest Motor Vehicle Use Map for roads and trails open to vehicles.
  • Do not hunt inside Yellowstone National Park or other areas closed to hunting. Know your boundaries.

Bears and safety

Idaho Fish and Game warns that grizzly bears may be found in all or parts of this area. Carry bear spray and know how to use it, store food and game carefully, and follow the agency's guidance. Weather and snow change fast at this elevation. See our best time to visit and conditions page.
