Henrys Fork fly fishing: Box Canyon and the Railroad Ranch

Short answer: The Henrys Fork of the Snake River runs through Island Park and is famous among fly fishers. Two stretches stand out: Box Canyon, a short, fast run of pocket water below Island Park Dam, and the Railroad Ranch in Harriman State Park, where the river slows into broad flats. You need an Idaho fishing license, and rules differ by section.

Box Canyon

Box Canyon is a short, fast stretch of pocket water just below Island Park Dam. Guides describe powerful rainbows and difficult wading because of the current. Many anglers report the salmonfly hatch beginning around early June, and Box Canyon holds fish year-round, though winter fishing is slower. Idaho Fish and Game says it does not require a permit to float Box Canyon. See the Box Canyon page on the Fishing Planner.

The Railroad Ranch at Harriman State Park

Downstream, the Henrys Fork slows through Harriman State Park, where anglers call the stretch "the Ranch." Guides describe it as fly-fishing water with special rules, and Idaho Fish and Game has listed a season running from mid-June through November on this section. Harriman charges a vehicle entry fee unless you hold a state parks pass. Rules and dates change, so verify before you go. Moose, elk and trumpeter swans are commonly seen along the river.

Plan your day

  • Use the Henrys Fork Foundation's upper river angler access map.
  • Flows from Island Park Dam change through the season, so check conditions before you go.
  • Do not assume Ranch rules apply in Box Canyon or elsewhere. Rules differ by section.
  • Hire a local guide if you are new to the river. Guides know the hatches, access and rules.
