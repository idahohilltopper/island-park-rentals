Warm River, Idaho: camping, tubing and fishing

Short answer: Warm River is a slow, spring-fed tributary of the Henrys Fork about 9 miles northeast of Ashton on Highway 47. The Forest Service says it is a popular place for tubing and fishing, and the Warm River Campground sits right on the water. The Forest Service says Island Park is nearby.

Getting there

New to the area? See our Ashton guide.

  • From Ashton, Idaho, travel northeast on Highway 47 to Warm River. Turn right onto Fish Creek Road and continue a quarter mile to the campground entrance (Forest Service).
  • Ashton is about 9 miles away. The campground sits at about 5,200 feet.
  • Highway 47 is the road our Mesa Falls guide describes. Upper Mesa Falls was reported closed for construction from August 2026, so check the Forest Service for current access before you plan a loop.

Warm River Campground

  • Season. The Forest Service and Recreation.gov say late May through early September. After that, it is weather dependent, first come first served, with no fee, no toilets and no water.
  • Sites. Recreation.gov lists one large group site for up to 100 guests and several single sites, 11 of them tent only. Reserve on Recreation.gov.
  • Facilities. Vault toilets, drinking water, trash collection and firewood for sale from the host. Off-road vehicles are not permitted in the campground.
  • The river. The Forest Service describes the slow-moving Warm River, lined with thickets of willow.

See also our camping in Island Park guide. Dates, fees and rules change, so confirm with the Forest Service or the Ashton/Island Park District Office.

Tubing and fishing

  • Tubing. The Forest Service calls Warm River a popular location for tubing and fishing. Check water and weather first, and wear a life jacket.
  • Fishing. Recreation.gov says anglers cast for whitefish, brook, rainbow and cutthroat trout. Idaho Fish and Game describes Warm River as a short, spring-fed Henrys Fork tributary that fishes well when other waters are off-color. See our Buffalo and Warm river fishing guide, and check Idaho Fish and Game for current rules and a license.

Hiking, biking and riding

The Forest Service says numerous hiking, mountain biking and horseback riding trails in the immediate area lead to natural springs, waterfalls and old railroad lines and tunnels. It does not name the trails here, so ask the district office for current trail conditions. Nearby, the 29.6-mile Ashton-Tetonia Trail follows the abandoned Union Pacific railroad grade from Ashton to Tetonia, with historic trestles at Fall River, Conant Creek and Bitch Creek, according to Idaho Parks and Recreation. A paving project is described, so check its status first.
