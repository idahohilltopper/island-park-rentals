Getting to Island Park, Idaho from Bozeman, Big Sky and Idaho Falls

Short answer: Most visitors fly into Bozeman Yellowstone International Airport or Idaho Falls Regional Airport and drive. Bozeman to West Yellowstone is about 90 miles (roughly 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours), and Island Park is about another 28 miles south on US 20. Idaho Falls to Island Park is roughly 80 to 90 miles on US 20, about 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 30 minutes.

From Bozeman

Bozeman Yellowstone International Airport is the closest large airport to the west side of Yellowstone. From the airport, US Highway 191 runs south through Gallatin Canyon to West Yellowstone, about 90 miles. From West Yellowstone, take US 20 south into Idaho. It crosses the Continental Divide at Targhee Pass (about 7,072 feet) and enters Island Park, where our Whitebark Cabin is about 38 miles from Yellowstone National Park. As a rough estimate, plan on about two and a half hours from the airport to the cabin without stops.

From Big Sky

Big Sky is about 47 to 48 miles north of West Yellowstone on US 191, about an hour. The road follows the Gallatin River through the canyon, and it is a good idea to download offline maps before you go. Island Park is farther south: add roughly 28 miles on US 20 from West Yellowstone, so plan on about an hour and a half to two hours each way as a rough estimate. Island Park suits groups who want winter snowmobiling and a private hot tub more than a daily ski commute. See our Island Park vs Big Sky vs West Yellowstone comparison.

From Idaho Falls

Idaho Falls Regional Airport is the main option on the Idaho side. US 20 climbs north through Rexburg, St. Anthony and Ashton into Island Park. Local guides put the drive at roughly 80 to 90 miles and about 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 30 minutes, with passing lanes along much of the Island Park corridor.

Winter travel and Yellowstone access

  • Winter is a wonderful time to visit. US 191 and US 20 are the main routes, so give yourself a little extra time and check road conditions before you leave, as you would for any mountain trip.
  • Yellowstone has a special winter season. For 2026 to 2027, the National Park Service lists guided snowmobile and snowcoach trips through the West Entrance from December 15, 2026 to March 15, 2027 (dates can change), and the road from the North Entrance at Gardiner to the Northeast Entrance stays open to cars. See the NPS Park Roads page.
  • Island Park is in full swing for winter, with groomed snowmobile trails right outside the cabin door. See the snowmobiling guide and winter in Island Park.
