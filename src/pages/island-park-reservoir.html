Island Park Reservoir: fishing, boating and dam history

Short answer: Island Park Reservoir covers 8,400 acres on the Henrys Fork, surrounded by lodgepole pine in the Caribou-Targhee National Forest. It is popular for boating, fishing and water skiing, and in winter for ice fishing. The dam that forms it was built between 1937 and 1939 and stores water for irrigation.

Boating and water skiing

The Forest Service lists boat ramps or docks at Buttermilk Campground, Island Park, Lakeside Lodge, McCrea Bridge Campground, Mill Creek and the West end. Motorized boating and water skiing are listed activities. Check the Forest Service for current ramp access and water conditions before you launch.

Fishing

  • What is in the water. Idaho Fish and Game says the reservoir is stocked with rainbow trout and kokanee. See the agency's Island Park Reservoir fishing planner for current details.
  • Winter. Idaho Fish and Game says anglers often ice fish near the dam for kokanee and rainbow trout. Ice thickness changes, so check conditions locally and fish only on safe ice.
  • Rules. An Idaho fishing license is required for anglers 14 and older. Check Idaho Fish and Game for current regulations and any special rules.
  • Below the dam. The Henrys Fork continues through Box Canyon. See our Henrys Fork fly fishing guide.

Camping

The Forest Service lists camping at Buttermilk, McCrea Bridge, Mill Creek and West End. Dates, fees and reservations change, so check before you go. See our camping in Island Park guide.

About the dam

  • Wikipedia says Island Park Dam was built between 1937 and 1939 as part of the Minidoka Project and is operated by the US Bureau of Reclamation.
  • The water is stored for irrigation and sent by the Cross Cut Canal to farms in Fremont and Madison counties in Idaho and Teton County in Wyoming. A hydroelectric plant was added in 1994.
  • Wikipedia says the Island Park and Grassy Lake reservoirs were built as an alternative to a larger project that would have flooded the Fall River area of Yellowstone National Park.

Plan your visit

  • The view. You can drive across the top of the dam for views of the Centennial Mountains and Box Canyon, per the Forest Service.
  • Water. The Forest Service says potable water is not available at the site, so bring your own.
  • Questions. Call the Caribou-Targhee National Forest Supervisor's Office at 208-557-5900 for current conditions and advisories.
  • Food. Lakeside Lodge is on the reservoir. See where we eat in Island Park.
