Pet-friendly cabin in Island Park, Idaho

Our Whitebark Cabin in Island Park, Idaho, welcomes pets. It sleeps 10, has a private hot tub, and is a short drive from Yellowstone. Before you book, check the Airbnb listing for the current pet policy, fees and house rules. Then read below, because dogs have limits in Yellowstone and in some local parks.

The cabin at a glance

  • Pets welcome. Confirm the policy and fees on Airbnb.
  • Sleeps up to 10, with three bedrooms.
  • Private hot tub, indoor fireplace and backyard fire pit.
  • In-unit washer and dryer, which helps after muddy trails.
  • Parking for small to medium trailers.

Where your dog can go near Island Park

PlacePet rule (as published)
Yellowstone National ParkPets only in developed areas, within 100 feet of roads, parking areas and campgrounds. Not on boardwalks, trails, in the backcountry or in thermal areas. Six-foot leash, or in a car or crate.
Harriman State ParkDogs and other pets only in parking lots next to vehicles, on a six-foot leash. No trail or road access.
Henrys Lake State ParkPets allowed on a six-foot leash.
Caribou-Targhee National ForestPets on leashes no longer than six feet in designated recreation areas. Check local rules for each campground and trail.

Rules change, so confirm with the National Park Service, Idaho Parks and Recreation and the forest before you go.

Planning Yellowstone days with a dog

  • The NPS says pets may not be left unattended or tied to objects, and may stay in vehicles only for short periods. It also says there are no kennels in the park, but boarding is available in nearby communities.
  • Check the cabin listing's house rules about leaving a pet alone before you plan long park days.
  • The NPS says its pet rules exist partly to protect pets from predators like bears and coyotes, and from hot springs. Keep dogs close and on a leash.
  • Some plants in Yellowstone campgrounds and developed areas are toxic to pets, including common yarrow, water hemlock and larkspur. The NPS says to seek medical attention immediately if a pet eats one.
  • Bag and dispose of pet waste.

Dog-friendly ideas close to the cabin

  • Sit by the backyard fire pit when fire restrictions allow.
  • Stop at Henrys Lake State Park, where pets are allowed on a six-foot leash.
  • Take a drive up Sawtell Peak in summer for the view. Keep dogs leashed and away from wildlife.
  • Check forest campgrounds and trailheads for their own rules first.
