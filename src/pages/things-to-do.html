Things to do in Island Park, Idaho

Short answer: Island Park is a year-round outdoors destination. In warm months, drive up Sawtell Peak, fish the Henrys Fork or Henrys Lake, walk Harriman State Park, see Big Springs and Mesa Falls, and day-trip into Yellowstone. In winter, ride snowmobiles on groomed trails or ski and snowshoe at Harriman. Tap any card to learn more.

New to the area? Start with our Island Park, Idaho travel guide.

Places to go in Island Park

Sawtell Peak

A drive from sagebrush meadows to an alpine summit, listed by the Forest Service at about 9,866 feet, with views of the Island Park caldera, Island Park Reservoir and Henrys Lake. Sawtell Peak Road (Forest Road 024) is well-graded gravel and turns west off Highway 20 about 1.5 miles north of Mack's Inn. It is closed from about November 1 to June 1, and the weather up top can change fast.

Forest Service: Sawtell Peak ↗

Fly fishing the Henrys Fork

The Henrys Fork of the Snake River runs through Island Park and is known among fly fishers worldwide. Box Canyon, just below Island Park Dam, is fast pocket water where wading can be hard; downstream the river slows through Harriman State Park. An Idaho fishing license is required. Check river flows and rules before you go.

Fishing guides: Henrys Fork, Henrys Lake, Madison →

Winter: snowmobiling and skiing

Island Park is one of the region's best-known snowmobiling areas. The Forest Service reports more than 500 miles of groomed trails across the Caribou-Targhee National Forest, and tourism sources cite more when nearby areas are included. Big Sky is a longer drive: about 47 to 48 miles north of West Yellowstone, and Jackson Hole is roughly 100 miles and two hours or more away. See our winter guide.

Mesa Falls

On the Henrys Fork east of US 20 and north of Ashton, Upper Mesa Falls drops about 114 feet and Lower Mesa Falls about 65 feet. Both are reached from the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway (Idaho Highway 47), roughly a 30-mile drive. Confirm current access with the Forest Service before you go.

Mesa Falls visitor guide →

Harriman State Park

Historic Railroad Ranch on the Henrys Fork, where moose, elk and trumpeter swans are commonly seen. In winter the park has groomed trails (roughly 25 miles by published figures) for classic and skate skiing, snowshoeing and fat biking.

Harriman visitor guide →

Henrys Lake

A roughly 6,000-acre lake ringed by mountains along the Continental Divide, known for trout including Yellowstone cutthroat and brook trout. Henrys Lake State Park has a lakeshore campground and a boat ramp.

Henrys Lake State Park ↗

Island Park Reservoir

A Bureau of Reclamation reservoir on the Henrys Fork, popular for boating, fishing and water skiing in summer and ice fishing in winter. The Forest Service cites roughly 8,400 acres. Check current water-quality advisories before you go.

Reservoir guide →

Big Springs

A first-magnitude spring in the Caribou-Targhee National Forest and one of the two main sources of the Henrys Fork, designated a National Natural Landmark in 1980. The Forest Service area has trails and picnic space and is popular in summer.

Big Springs visitor guide →

Big Springs Water Trail

A National Recreation Water Trail that starts near Big Springs and ends about 4.5 miles away at Mack's Inn. Recreation.gov describes a lazy float of about three hours, and some paddlers plan on four. Big Springs Campground has been affected by construction, so verify access before you plan.

Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center

An AZA-accredited, not-for-profit wildlife park next to Yellowstone's West Entrance in West Yellowstone, home to grizzly bears, gray wolves and other rescued wildlife. Hours change with the seasons.

National forest and state park campgrounds along the Henrys Fork, Island Park Reservoir and Henrys Lake. Dates, reservations and closures change every year.
