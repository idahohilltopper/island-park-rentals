Where to stay near Yellowstone: Island Park or West Yellowstone?

Short answer: Stay in West Yellowstone if you want to walk to restaurants and be minutes from the gate. Stay in Island Park if you want a private cabin, more space for a group, a hot tub and a quieter evening, in exchange for a longer drive. Our Whitebark Cabin is about 38 miles from Yellowstone National Park, roughly a 30 to 45 minute drive to the west entrance area.

The drive from Island Park to the west entrance

Island Park and West Yellowstone are connected by US Highway 20, which crosses the Continental Divide at Targhee Pass (about 7,072 feet) just before West Yellowstone. Published distances from Island Park to West Yellowstone range from about 33 to 44 miles, and drive times run roughly 45 minutes to an hour depending on where in Island Park you start. Add time in summer for traffic at the entrance and wildlife stops.

Yellowstone in winter: a snowy adventure

Winter is a beautiful time to see Yellowstone, with a snow-covered landscape and a quieter feel. The park runs a special winter season. For 2026 to 2027, the National Park Service lists guided snowmobile and snowcoach trips through the West Entrance from December 15, 2026 to March 15, 2027 (dates can change), and the road between the North Entrance at Gardiner and the Northeast Entrance at Cooke City stays open to cars. West Yellowstone is the classic starting point for guided trips, and Island Park is about 28 miles away, so you can ride Island Park's groomed trails one day and head into the park with a guide the next. Check the NPS Park Roads page for current dates, and see our winter in Island Park guide.

Why groups pick Island Park

  • One cabin for up to 10 people instead of several hotel rooms (Whitebark Cabin).
  • A kitchen and laundry make longer stays easier.
  • Pets are welcome at the Whitebark Cabin.
  • Fly fishing, lakes, trails and winter riding are close to the door. See things to do in Island Park.

Tips for the drive

  • Start early in summer, when park traffic and wildlife jams slow things down.
  • In winter, check park road and entrance status before you go.
  • Fill the tank and pack snacks. Services are sparse inside the park.
