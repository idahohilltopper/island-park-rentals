St. Anthony Sand Dunes: visitor guide

The St. Anthony Sand Dunes are 10,600 acres of white quartz sand with dunes up to 400 feet high, managed by the Bureau of Land Management near St. Anthony, Idaho. They are popular for off-road riding, and a day-use fee applies. Big parts of the area close in winter for wildlife. They make a good summer or fall day trip from Island Park.

At a glance

What to do

  • Ride the dunes. The BLM says the area is popular for high off-road vehicle use. See our side-by-side and ATV guide for Idaho rules. The BLM brochures and safety video cover where to ride and safety guidelines.
  • Explore on foot or horse. The BLM notes some visitors prefer to explore on horse and on foot.
  • Rentals. Visit Idaho says off-road vehicle rentals are available in nearby St. Anthony, and Yellowstone Teton Territory lists local outfitters for rentals and guided tours.
  • Camp at Egin Lakes. Reserve early for busy weekends.

Winter closures and wildlife

Visit Idaho says most of the dunes are closed during the winter for wildlife migration. The BLM gives these dates:

  • The big dunes on the west side, west of Egin Lakes Campground, are closed from January 1 through sunrise on April 1.
  • The area south of Egin-Hamer Road is closed January 1 through sunrise April 1.
  • The areas completely surrounding the open sand are closed from January 1 through sunrise on May 1.
  • The smaller dunes east of Egin Lakes Campground to Red Road are open year-round, but are usually covered in snow by January 1.

Please check the BLM's current closure map before you go. The BLM also asks riders to avoid damage to vegetation and impacts to wildlife. Much of the area is managed as a wilderness study area.

Planning from Island Park

  • None of the sources we read give a distance from Island Park. Check a map and allow a full day.
  • Bring water, sun protection and layers. Sand and weather can change fast.
  • Combine the dunes with Mesa Falls or the Henrys Fork on another day. See our things to do.
  • Our Whitebark Cabin sleeps 10 and has trailer parking, which suits groups with toys.

Sources: Bureau of Land Management, Visit Idaho and Wikipedia. Rules, fees and closures change, so confirm with the BLM before you go.
