Madison River fishing near West Yellowstone

The Madison River is one of the best-known trout rivers in the Rockies, and it is a short drive from Island Park through West Yellowstone. It flows through Yellowstone National Park, into Hebgen Lake and below the dam into Quake Lake, then down the famous Upper Madison. Inside the park you need a Yellowstone fishing permit, and in Montana you need a Montana license.

The stretches anglers talk about

  • Yellowstone National Park water: guides describe this as mostly a spring and fall fishery, since park water warms in summer. One guide says many locals consider October the best month, when large trout from Hebgen Lake move into the river.
  • Hebgen Dam to Quake Lake: water released from the bottom of Hebgen Lake stays cold through the summer. Quake Lake itself was formed by an earth slide during a major earthquake in 1959, according to Montana Fish, Wildlife and Parks.
  • Quake Lake to Ennis Lake (the Upper Madison): the stretch many people mean when they talk about the Madison, and a popular drift-boat and wade fishery.

Licenses and rules

  • Yellowstone National Park: anglers 16 and older need a park fishing permit, available through Recreation.gov. State licenses are not valid or required for park waters. The standard season runs from the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend through October 31, and the NPS has extended the season on parts of the Madison, Firehole and Gibbon rivers to open May 1. See the NPS fishing page and the 2026 regulations.
  • Montana waters: a Montana fishing license. Montana Fish, Wildlife and Parks lists regulations and waterbody information on its Madison River page.
  • Park and state rules are separate, and many sections have special restrictions, so check both.

Madison River fishing report: where to check conditions

Conditions change daily with flows and temperatures. Check current closures and restrictions on the NPS fishing page and Montana Fish, Wildlife and Parks' waterbody closures page. In hot, low-water periods, agencies sometimes limit fishing to protect trout, including Montana's hoot-owl restrictions that close afternoon fishing, so early starts pay off. Local fly shops and guides also post regular reports.

From Island Park
