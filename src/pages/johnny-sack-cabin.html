Johnny Sack Cabin at Big Springs

Short answer: Johnny Sack Cabin is a historic log bungalow beside Big Springs, near Island Park, Idaho. It was built in the early 1930s by Johnny Sack, a German immigrant cabinetmaker, and is known for its split-bark ornamentation and handmade furniture. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. A short paved path leads to it from the Big Springs campground.

The story

  • The builder. Johnny Sack was a German immigrant who worked as a carpenter or cabinetmaker. One local history says he was only 4 feet 11 inches tall, and that his basement workshop was built to his size.
  • The land. He leased a small piece of Forest Service land at Big Springs in 1929. The Idaho Heritage Trust says the cabin was built in strict violation of his $4.15 land lease agreement.
  • The dates. Wikipedia gives construction as 1932 to 1934, and another local history says he built it between 1932 and 1935. The Idaho Heritage Trust describes about three years of building around 1929. Sources differ, so we say the early 1930s.
  • Home. The Idaho Heritage Trust says he made it his permanent home for nearly 30 years. After his death in 1957 the cabin was due to be removed, but local people worked to save it.

What makes it special

  • Split-bark design. Wikipedia says the cabin is noted for using split bark in the intricate ornamentation of the cabin and the furniture inside it. Bark was left on the wood as texture and color on trim, wall panels, lamps, chairs and beds he made himself.
  • The building. Wikipedia describes a very well-made log bungalow, about 20 by 27 feet, with porches as extensions.
  • The contents. The Idaho Heritage Trust says the interior holds furniture Sack made and a collection of his hand tools.
  • The waterwheel house. The original wheelhouse stands nearby and once generated electricity for the cabin. Whether the wheel turns today is not stated in the sources we read.
  • Recognition. The cabin is on the National Register of Historic Places. In 2024 it was the subject of the Idaho Heritage Trust's Frederick Walters Award for Excellence in Historic Preservation, recognizing the Forest Service and Fremont County for maintaining the cabin and waterwheel.

Visiting

  • Where: beside Big Springs, in the Caribou-Targhee National Forest. Recreation.gov gives directions to the Big Springs area: from Mack's Inn on US 20, turn east on Big Springs Loop Road and drive about 4.5 miles. See our Big Springs guide.
  • Walking: a short paved path leads to the cabin from the Big Springs campground.
  • Hours: Wikipedia says the cabin is open to visitors, but we could not confirm the current season or hours. The Idaho Heritage Trust says the building is owned by the US Forest Service and managed by Fremont County, so confirm with the Forest Service or the county before you go.

Pair it with the spring itself, a Henrys Fork fishing day or Harriman State Park. See more in our Island Park guide.
